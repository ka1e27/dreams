## 2. Gaps that matter — ranked

### 2.1 🔴 INA228 current sensing is non-functional as drawn

All three INA228s have **`Vin+`, `Vin-` and `Vbus` unconnected**, and there are **no shunt resistors
anywhere in the design**. An INA228 measures current as the differential voltage across an external
shunt; with no shunt and no connections it measures nothing.

Needed: **3 shunt resistors**, one per domain, sized to the domain's current — the drive pair is
~90 A per `CLAUDE.md`, so sub-milliohm with a real power rating; electronics and winch are far lower.
`Vbus` also needs to land on the rail being monitored.

**And all three share one I²C address.** `A0`/`A1` are unconnected on every one, so they must be
strapped to three distinct combinations or only one will ever answer.

### 2.2 🔴 No decoupling capacitors on any IC

The only capacitors on the board are `C1`–`C3` (bulk) and `C4`–`C7` (ADC filters). There is **no
100 nF decoupling** on `U2`, `U3`, `U4`, `U5`, `U7`, `U8`, `U9`–`U11`, `A1` or `A2`. Every one of those
datasheets asks for it.

Worst case in that list: **`U4` BNO085 pin 9 `CAP` is unconnected.** That is not decoupling — it is the
required external capacitor for the part's internal regulator. The BNO085 will not work without it.

### 2.3 🔴 BNO085 strap pins are floating — the exact V2 failure

`PS0/WAKE`, `PS1`, `SA0/H_MOSI` (address), `~H_CS`, `~RST` and `~H_INT` are all unconnected.

`CLAUDE.md` and the journal archive record V2 losing a long debugging arc to precisely this: reads
worked, the address ACKed, and every payload write was refused because the module's interface pins
were floating. PS1=0/PS0=0 selects I²C; ADDR to GND gives 0x4A. **Strap them deliberately.**

### 2.4 🟠 The winch has no power path

`J14` brings in the winch M18 pack and `J10` carries the ESC PWM signal — but nothing connects the
pack to the ESC. `CLAUDE.md` describes the V2 chain as
`1× M18 ─► WINCH ─[20A]─► MOSFET kill → winch ESC`. There is **no MOSFET, no gate drive and no power
output** for the winch ESC in V3.

### 2.5 🟠 Pod tether schottky diode is missing

`V3_PCB_SESSION_CONTEXT.md` is explicit: a schottky in series between the tether 5 V and the XIAO's
5V/VBUS pin, because that pin is shared with the module's USB/charge circuitry. The `D_SOD-123`
footprint is in the library but **no diode is placed**, and `U12`'s 5 V pin sits directly on `+5V` —
exactly the backfeed the diode exists to prevent.

### 2.6 🟠 A4988 is unusable as drawn

- `VMOT` unconnected, and the A4988 needs a **bulk electrolytic right at VMOT** (47–100 µF).
- `1A/1B/2A/2B` unconnected on both the driver *and* `J5` — `J5` is the only part on the sheet with
  **every pin unconnected**.
- `STEP`, `DIR`, `~ENABLE`, `~SLEEP`, `~RESET`, `MS1`–`MS3` unconnected. `~SLEEP` and `~RESET` are
  normally tied together.

### 2.7 🟡 Teensy power input needs confirming

`U1` has both a `VIN` and a `5V` pin in this symbol. `5V` is on the `+5V` net; **`VIN` is
unconnected.** Confirm which pad actually is the Teensy 4.1's supply input and that it is fed.

Related: powering a Teensy 4.1 from an external 5 V rail while USB may also be connected requires
**cutting the VUSB/VIN pad trace on the Teensy**. That is a physical mod, not a schematic item, but it
belongs in the build notes.

### 2.8 🟡 Sensor I²C is not wired to the mux

`U2`'s eight channel pairs (`SD0`–`SD7`, `SC0`–`SC7`) are all unconnected, as is every sensor's
SDA/SCL. This is the bulk of the remaining wiring.

⚠ **Each channel you use needs its own pull-up pair.** Only the upstream pair (`R9`/`R10`) exists — a
mux isolates the channels, so the upstream pull-ups do not serve them.

### 2.9 🟡 Sense chains have no ADC pin

`ELEC_ADC`, `WINCH_ADC`, `DRIVE_ADC`, `S12_ADC` each hold a 1 k and a 100 nF and stop there. Four
Teensy analog pins needed.

### 2.10 🟡 Drive domain has no input

`+VDRIVE` has one member (`R1.1`). Only elec and winch XT60s were requested. If the drive pack is
entirely off-board — plausible, since the VESCs are — then the drive divider on this board has nothing
to measure and should either get an input or be dropped.

## 7. UPDATE 3 — current sensing complete (2026-08-05)

Currents supplied: **each drive motor 100 A max / 45 A rated (two motors)**, electronics ~5 A,
winch ~20 A. **87 components, 71 named nets.** Five passes clean, and `isolated_pin_label` and

### ⚠ The drive shunt cannot go on this board

The drive pack never reaches the carrier: only the elec and winch XT60s exist, and the VESCs are
off-board on CAN. Putting a 100 A shunt on this PCB would mean routing 100 A of pack current through
it for no reason.

So `U9` senses an **off-board** shunt through **`J15`**, a 4-pin JST-XH: `1 GND` (drive return / star
point) · `2 VDRIVE` · `3 SH+` · `4 SH-`. That connector also finally gives `+VDRIVE` a source, which
closes §2.10 — the drive divider R1/R2 now has something to measure.

### On-board shunts

Four-terminal Kelvin, `Device:R_Shunt` on `R_Shunt_Vishay_WSK2512_6332Metric_T1.19mm`. Pack current
passes through the shunt before it becomes the rail:

| Ref | Domain | Value | Chain | At max | % of FS | Power |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R32 | electronics | **10 mΩ** | J13 → R32 → +VELEC | 50 mV @ 5 A | 30.5 % | 0.25 W |
| R33 | winch | **2 mΩ** | J14 → R33 → +VWINCH | 40 mV @ 20 A | 24.4 % | 0.80 W |
| — | drive (external) | **0.25 mΩ** suggested | off-board, sensed via J15 | 25 mV @ 100 A | 15.3 % | 2.50 W |

INA228 full scale is ±163.84 mV, so all three sit comfortably inside it. The 20-bit ADC means a low
shunt value costs almost nothing in resolution — at 0.25 mΩ the LSB is still ~1.25 mA — which is why
the drive shunt is sized for heat rather than sensitivity. Dropping the INA228 to its ±40.96 mV range
gains 4× resolution on all three if wanted.

⚠ **Pad roles on the WSK2512 are 1/4 current, 2/3 sense** — not 1/2 and 3/4. The current pads are
4.65 mm² and the sense pads 1.06 mm². The `R_Shunt` symbol agrees (pins 1/4 are 5.08 mm apart on the
through axis; 2/3 branch off). I had this backwards in the first draft of the symbol description and
corrected it.

⚠ **Kelvin routing is a layout obligation the netlist cannot express.** Pads 1 and 2 sit on the same
net, as do 3 and 4, so nothing stops the router taking the INA228 sense trace off the *current* pad.
At 2 mΩ a couple of milliohms of shared trace is a large error. Route sense to the sense pads.

## 8. UPDATE 4 — winch kill path + ESC power output (2026-08-05)

**94 components, 76 named nets.** Five passes clean. This closes §2.4, the last structural gap.

### Topology — high-side P-FET, fail-safe OFF

```
+VWINCH ──┬────────[Q1 P-FET  S→D]──── WINCH_SW ──► J16 (XT60 to winch ESC)
          │              │ G
       [R34 10k]─────────┤
                         │
                    [R35 10k]
                         │
                    Q1 gate pulled toward GND by Q2 when killed
                    Q2: D=WINCH_GDRV, S=GND, G=WINCH_KILL
                    R36 10k gate pull-down · R37 1k from Teensy pin 29
```

**J16** is an XT60 power *output* to the winch ESC — the part V2 had as `J6 "4s output"` and V3 was
missing. High-side switching, so the ESC return is common ground.

### Why high-side, and why fail-safe falls out of it

Low-side N-channel would be easier to drive and far more efficient, but it puts the switch in the
return path — which breaks the isolated-domains-with-one-star-point arrangement and leaves the ESC
ground floating when off.

With the high-side P-FET, **R34 pulls the gate to source whenever Q2 isn't driven**, so:

- MCU in reset, high-Z, or unprogrammed → R36 holds Q2 off → R34 holds Q1 off → **winch dead**.
- There is no state where a hung MCU can energise the winch.

That is the whole point. The journal's 2026-07-31 entry records the drive ESCs holding their last PWM
through `SET_THR 0,0` *and* through `ESTOP` — a signal-path stop is not a stop. This is a power-path
cut that does not depend on the ESC behaving.

### Gate drive numbers (M18 winch pack, 21 V max)

| | |
|---|---|
| V<sub>GS</sub> with Q2 on | **−10.5 V** = −21 × R34/(R34+R35) — fully enhanced, inside the ±20 V limit |
| V<sub>GS</sub> with Q2 off | **0 V** → OFF |
| Divider current when on | 1.05 mA |
| Q1 dissipation at 20 A | **2.0 W** at 5 mΩ · **4.0 W** at 10 mΩ |

The R34/R35 divider is what keeps V<sub>GS</sub> in range, so **no Zener is needed**. Q1 needs
≥40 V V<sub>DS</sub> for a 21 V pack and Rds(on) chosen against the heatsink — TO-220 vertical, so a
tab heatsink is available.

### Verified topology

Every assertion checked against the netlist: Q1 source on the rail · drain feeds **only** the ESC
output (so the switch really is in series, not bypassing) · R34 pulls to **source not ground** · Q2
source grounded · R36 pulls Q2's gate to ground · the MCU reaches Q2 through R37 rather than directly ·
ESC return grounded.

### ⚠ The precedence question is still yours

Your README lists "leak detection with auto pod retraction". This kill cuts winch power, so it can
*prevent* that retraction. Nothing in hardware resolves that — the firmware must give retract logic
priority over the kill. Worth writing down before the winch is ever used in anger.

### Output capacitance — added

`C26` **470 µF 35 V** electrolytic and `C27` **100 nF** ceramic, both across `WINCH_SW` → GND, i.e.
downstream of Q1 on the ESC feed. `C2` sits upstream on `+VWINCH` and does nothing for the switched
side. `WINCH_SW` now carries Q1.2 · C26.1 · C27.1 · J16.1.

35 V on an 18 V nominal / 21 V max M18 pack is a comfortable ~1.7× margin. The electrolytic takes the
inductive kick from the ESC leads when Q1 opens under load; the ceramic handles what the electrolytic's
ESR and lead inductance cannot.

⚠ **Inrush is the trade.** Q1 now switches into 470 µF plus whatever the ESC's own input bank holds —
often several hundred µF more. The slow gate drive helps by accident: 10 kΩ into the P-FET's gate
charge gives a turn-on of tens of microseconds, so this is a soft start rather than a hard step. Still
worth checking the chosen FET's SOA against that surge, since it is the one place Q1 sees high current
and high V<sub>DS</sub> simultaneously. If it looks marginal, raise R35 to slow turn-on further.

## 9. UPDATE 5 — decisions closed (2026-08-05)

### 9.1 Leak auto-retract takes priority over the winch kill — DECIDED

The kill line (`WINCH_KILL`, Teensy pin 29) is software-controlled, so this is purely a firmware
precedence rule and needs no hardware change:

> **Leak detection must be able to override the winch kill.** On a pod-leak event the firmware
> asserts the kill only *after* retraction completes, never before. No code path may latch the kill
> in a way that blocks retract.

Two implementation notes for whoever writes the Teensy firmware:

- The kill is **fail-safe OFF**, so an MCU reset mid-retract drops the winch. Retract therefore has to
  be re-commanded on boot if a leak is still asserted, rather than assumed to have finished.
- This is the one place where "safest" is *not* "cut power". Killing the winch with the pod deployed
  and water coming in leaves the pod down. Document it next to the ESTOP semantics so the two
  failsafes are not confused.

### 9.2 Drive shunt part — Vishay WSBS8518, 250 µΩ

Specs below are from the WSBS8518…20 datasheet (Vishay doc 30341, rev 26-May-2026), text extracted
directly rather than recalled:

| | |
|---|---|
| Resistance | **250 µΩ** (ordering code `L2500` = 0.000250 Ω) |
| Power rating | **36 W** |
| Construction | Power Metal Strip, **two sense pins** — true 4-terminal Kelvin |
| Max current | √(P/R) = √(36 / 0.00025) ≈ **379 A** |
| TCR | ±110 ppm/°C at 250 µΩ |
| Tolerance | 5 % or 10 % |
| Thermal EMF | < 3 µV/°C |
| Operating range | −65 to +170 °C |
| Overload | 5× rated power for 5 s |

Against this design:

| Condition | Drop | Dissipation | % of 36 W |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90 A continuous (2 × 45 A rated) | 22.5 mV | 2.03 W | **5.6 %** |
| 100 A | 25.0 mV | 2.50 W | 6.9 % |
| 200 A (both motors at 100 A peak) | 50.0 mV | 10.0 W | 28 % |

So the thermal question is answered: at the real continuous operating point it runs at **under 6 % of
rating**, and even the theoretical 200 A both-motors-peak case is inside it — though the ~90 A pack
limit `CLAUDE.md` records means that case cannot actually occur.

⚠ **Tolerance is the accuracy limit, not the thermals.** 5 % on the shunt is 5 % on every current
reading. The INA228 has a `SHUNT_CAL` register — measure the installed shunt (or calibrate against a
known load) and program the real value rather than trusting the marking. The ±110 ppm/°C TCR is minor
next to that.

Vishay's own datasheet header points at the **WSBE** series as the superior-TCR upgrade if better
accuracy is wanted.

To be explicit about what is and is not on this board: **no drive pack power reaches the carrier at

rail voltage for sensing plus the Kelvin sense pair and the star-point return. That is intended, not a
gap.

## 10. UPDATE 6 — 🔴 drive divider was over-ranging the ADC (2026-08-05)

Asking where the drive ADC voltage comes in prompted a re-check of the divider maths, which turned up
a defect that would have destroyed a Teensy input.

### What was wrong

`R1/R2` were **56k/10k** — the as-built value `CLAUDE.md` records for V2. But V2's drive domain is
**2× M18 in parallel, 21 V max**, while V3's is **3× M18 in series, 63 V max** (the same 63 V figure
`V3_PCB_SESSION_CONTEXT.md` cites as the reason INA226 was rejected for INA228).

| | ratio | V at ADC | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 56k/10k on a 21 V rail (V2) | 6.60 | 3.18 V | fits |
| 56k/10k on a **63 V** rail (V3, as drawn) | 6.60 | **9.55 V** | 🔴 **2.9× over a 3.3 V pin** |
| **220k/10k** (fixed) | 23.00 | **2.74 V** | ok, 17 % headroom |

`R1` is now **220k**, `R2` stays 10k. 220k was already in the BOM (R7), so no new value. Thévenin
9.6 kΩ, comfortable behind the 1 k + 100 nF filter. Over-range voltage is now 76 V, well above the
63 V rail.

This is precisely the V2→V3 assumption error `docs/v3/README.md` exists to warn about — a value copied
from the as-built board without re-checking the thing it depends on. It was introduced here, and the
fence document did not stop it.

### ⚠ Elec and winch dividers are tight — not changed, but specify 1 % parts

Both are 56k/10k on a 21 V rail → **3.18 V of a 3.3 V ceiling, 3.6 % headroom.** With 5 % resistors the
ratio can shift ~10 %, putting the worst case at **3.50 V — over the rail.**

Left at the as-built values because V2 has been running them, but **specify 1 % resistors on all four
dividers.** If you would rather have real margin, 68k/10k gives 2.69 V at 21 V (18 % headroom) for one
extra BOM value.

### Where the drive ADC voltage comes from

It already arrives on a JST-XH: **`J15` is `JST_XH_B4B-XH-A_1x04`**, and pin 2 (`VDRIVE`) feeds R1 →
R2 → R13 → `DRIVE_ADC` → Teensy A2. `+VDRIVE` carries `J15.2 · R1.1 · U9 Vbus`, so one connector
serves both the voltage divider and the INA228's bus-voltage input.

If the voltage tap and the shunt are at physically different points on the pack, that 4-pin can be
split into a 2-pin for GND+VDRIVE and a 3-pin for the Kelvin pair — say so and it is a small edit.

|---|---|---|---|---|

1. **Same rail** → drop one chain. That frees a connector, 2 resistors, a cap and an ADC pin.

Worth deciding before layout. Nothing is wrong as drawn either way — it is redundancy, not an error.

## 14. UPDATE 9 — independent five-pass audit; four blockers found and fixed (2026-08-05)

Re-review driven off `kicad-cli sch erc --severity-all` and an exported netlist, with every strap and
power-on default checked against the actual datasheet rather than against this document. **89 → 99
components.** Four of the findings would each have stopped the board working; one was a safety
defect in the winch kill path.

### 14.1 🔴 BNO085 was strapped for a crystal that was never fitted

`CLKSEL0` → GND does **not** select the internal oscillator. Datasheet Figure 1-8:

| Source | CLKSEL0 | CLKSEL1 |
|---|---|---|
| Crystal | **0 or unconnected** | connected to crystal |
| External | 1 | 1 |
| Internal | **1** | 0 or unconnected |

So `CLKSEL0` = 0 selected **crystal** mode while `XIN32`/`XOUT32` were left floating — the 32 kHz
oscillator could never start and the IMU would not run. §5's "CLKSEL0 → GND" was the wrong strap for
a crystal-less design.

Resolved by fitting the crystal (the user's call over strapping `CLKSEL0` high), which makes the
existing GND strap correct. Per **Figure 1-7**, the reference circuit is the crystal across pins
26/27 **plus 22 pF from each pin to GND**:

- `Y1` 32.768 kHz, **CL = 12.5 pF**, 50 ppm — `Crystal_SMD_3215-2Pin_3.2x1.5mm` (Epson FC-135 /
  Abracon ABS07 land pattern, copied from KiCad's stock library rather than hand-authored)
- `C28`/`C29` **22 pF C0G** on `IMU_XIN`/`IMU_XOUT` to GND

The two 22 pF in series (11 pF) plus stray ≈ the 12.5 pF CL the crystal is specified for — which
reconciles the "22 pF" in older datasheet revisions with the "12.5 pF" in v1.17. They describe
different things; both numbers are needed.

⚠ Layout: `Y1` and its caps must sit hard against U4 pins 26/27 with a ground guard. This is a
high-impedance 32 kHz node and it is the one part of this schematic where placement is not optional.

### 14.2 🔴 The winch kill shared a UART with SBUS — and the shared pin defeats the fail-safe

`WINCH_KILL_MCU` was on Teensy **pin 29 = TX7**; SBUS is on **pin 28 = RX7**. Same `Serial7`.
`Serial7.begin()` claims both pins and **a UART TX idles high**, so initialising SBUS would drive
the kill line high → Q2 on → P-FET on → **the winch energises**. §8's whole "no state where a hung
MCU can energise the winch" argument fails at `Serial7.begin()`.

Moved to **pin 28 = D36**, which was unconnected and has no peripheral overlap.

Every other UART pairing in the design was already correct — D34/D35 (Serial8) for the pod tether,
D0/D1 (Serial1) for GPS. D29 was the single slip, and it landed on the one net where it mattered.

### 14.3 🔴 The pod schottky was fitted backwards

`D1` pin 1 (**K**) was on `+5V` and pin 2 (**A**) on `POD_5V` — the exact reverse of what the
symbol's own Description specifies ("Anode = carrier +5V, cathode = POD_5V"). As drawn it blocked
5 V reaching the pod and permitted the backfeed the diode exists to stop. Fixed by mirroring the
symbol, so the drawn cathode bar now points at `POD_5V` too.

### 14.4 🔴 The XIAO's 3V3 regulator output was tied to the carrier's +3V3

`U12.12` is the module's regulator **output**, so bonding it to `+3V3` back-drives it from the
Teensy's LDO — the same mistake §5 correctly avoided on the Pi's 3V3 pins. It is also physically
impossible: `J2` is a 4-wire tether (GND / 5V / TX / RX) with no 3V3 conductor. Removed, with a
`no_connect` in its place.

### 14.5 🟠 Seven pins had no defined state while the Teensy is high-Z

All undefined at power-on, in reset, and in the bootloader. Added **R38–R44, 10 k to +3V3**:

| Net | Why |
|---|---|
| `MUX_RST` | TCA9548A datasheet: *"The RESET input must be connected to VCC through a pull-up resistor."* |
| `IMU_RST` | BNO085 `NRST` has no internal pull |
| `STEP_EN` | Pololu confirm internal pulldowns on MS1–MS3 **only**; ENABLE has none, so the A4988's outputs came up undefined and the coils could energise before firmware ran. V2's own notes asked for this pull-up; it was not carried into V3 |
| `STEP_SLEEP` | same — no internal pull |
| `PWR_GOOD` | open-drain output with no pull-up; V2 had `R1` for this and V3 dropped it |
| `ENV_SCL`, `ENV_SDA` | BNO085 datasheet: *"This interface should be pulled up via resistors regardless of the presence of the external sensor as the SW polls for sensors at reset."* Not optional — the part polls this bus every reset |

### 14.7 Still open — not fixed, needs decisions

Ranked. None of these stop the board working, but the first three are the ones that would bite on
the water.

1. **+5V budget and protection.** One 5.5 A module feeds Pi 5 + pod tether + SBUS RX + WS2812 +
   Teensy + TJA1051 with only `C23` (10 µF electrolytic) and `C9` (100 nF), **no fuse, no
   soft-start**. Two USB cameras plus a SIM7600G-H (~2 A transmit bursts) can reach the limit. Add
   real bulk at the Pi header and budget it explicitly.
2. **The pod tether's 5 V is unfused and shares the Pi's rail.** A flooded pod — the failure the
   leak sensors exist to detect — shorts the rail that powers the Pi. Wants an eFuse or polyfuse.
3. **Pi USB-C backfeed.** The Pololu buck is not reverse-current tolerant and the Pi's header 5 V
   pins become outputs on USB-C. Same hazard V2 had. Pick one source or add ORing.
4. **`A2` pad map is self-flagged as uncertain** — the symbol says pads 11/12 (EN/PG) may be
   swapped. If they are, `PWR_GOOD` sits on **EN** and driving it low kills the board's own 5 V,
   Teensy included. Verify against the physical module; keep that pin input-only in firmware.
5. **Free reverse-polarity protection is unused.** Pololu: VRP *"provides access to the input
   voltage after reverse-voltage protection."* `+VELEC` taps raw VIN, so A4988 VMOT, C1, C25 and the
   divider are unprotected. Tap them from VRP (pins 9/10, currently unconnected) instead.
6. **No ESD/TVS on any of 10 external connectors** — worst are the hull-leak probes and the
   submerged tether, both straight to MCU pins.
7. **WS2812 driven at 3.3 V from a 5 V part.** V<sub>IH</sub> = 0.7 × V<sub>DD</sub> = 3.5 V, so
   out of spec. No series resistor, no local bulk at the LED.
8. **SBUS receiver powered at 5 V**, output level unspecified. The Teensy 4.1 is not 5 V tolerant.
9. **`J11`'s 2-pin JST-XH bonds the 12S pack negative to board GND.** If the VESCs' main negative
   opens, drive return tries to flow through that connector.
10. **BOM has no MPN, manufacturer or tolerance on any line** — including `Q1`, both shunts, and the
    dividers. §10's "specify 1 % on all four dividers" is still not captured anywhere, and elec/winch
    sit at 3.18 V of a 3.30 V ceiling.
11. **79 ERC errors with no NC flags.** A new real error will hide in that noise. Placing explicit
    no-connects on the genuinely-unused pins would make ERC a working check again.
12. Lower priority: `BOOTN` hard-tied where the datasheet asks for 10 k (blocks DFU); ADXL375 wants
    1 µF + 0.1 µF on Vs; 10 µF electrolytics are poor bulk for digital rails; the XIAO tether uses
    GPIO43/44 = ESP32-S3 UART0, so boot-log text lands on the Teensy; the 12S divider's 12.3 kΩ
    Thévenin is above the ADC's comfortable source impedance.

### 14.8 Firmware consequences of this pass

- **`WINCH_KILL` is now Teensy pin 28 (D36), not 29.** Nothing may configure `Serial7` TX.
- **`STEP_EN` and `STEP_SLEEP` now idle HIGH.** `~ENABLE` high = outputs disabled and `~SLEEP` high =
  awake, so the stepper comes up de-energised and the firmware must actively pull `STEP_EN` low to
  drive. That is the intended fail-safe, but it inverts the boot behaviour §6 assumed.
- **`PWR_GOOD` now reads a real logic high** instead of floating.

## 15. UPDATE 10 — pod off-board, 5 V rail protected (2026-08-05)

Two changes: the pod electronics leave this schematic, and the 5 V rail gets the branch protection
and bulk it was missing. **99 → 103 components.**

### 15.1 The pod is a separate board now

`U12` (XIAO ESP32-S3) is deleted along with its GND, its `no_connect` and its three labels. The pod
connects through **`J2` only** — the existing 4-pin JST-XH (GND / 5V / TX / RX). `TETHER_A` and

### 15.2 Every 5 V branch that leaves the board is now fused

The failure that mattered: the pod tether shares the rail that powers the Pi 5, so a flooded pod —
the exact event the leak sensors exist to detect — would brown out the whole boat. Fixed by
containment, one PTC per external branch:

```
                    ┌─[F1 0.75A]─── POD_5V_F ──[D1 ▷|]── POD_5V ──► J2  pod tether
+5V ────┬───────────┼─[F2 1.25A]─── LED_5V ─────────────────────► J6  status LED
        │           └─[F3 0.75A]─── SBUS_5V ────────────────────► J7  SBUS receiver
        ├── J12.2 / J12.4  Raspberry Pi 5
        ├── U1.48 Teensy VIN · U3.3 TJA1051 VCC
        └── C23 10µF · C9 100nF · C30 470µF · C31 100nF
```

Bel Fuse 0ZRE radial THT, matching the board's through-hole style. A short on any one harness now
trips its own fuse instead of collapsing the rail.

⚠ The pod sees roughly **4.5 V**, not 5 V — schottky V<sub>f</sub> ≈ 0.35 V plus the PTC's series
resistance, before any tether IR drop. Fine for the XIAO's onboard regulator, but size the tether
conductors with that budget in mind rather than assuming 5 V at the far end.

⚠ F3 is deliberately loose. An SBUS receiver draws well under 100 mA, so 0.75 A hold is ~8× margin —
chosen so the RC link, which is the manual override, can never nuisance-trip.

### 15.3 Bulk at the Pi

(10 µF electrolytic) and `C9` (100 nF) for a load that includes a Pi 5.

⚠ **Layout obligation:** C30/C31 are drawn on the rail but must be placed physically at `J12`'s 5 V
pins. Bulk at the regulator does nothing for transients at the far end of the trace.

### 15.4 Still open on this rail — needs a decision, not an edit

- **USB-C backfeed is not fixed.** The Pololu buck is not reverse-current tolerant and the Pi's

  ORing controller. Until then this is a **build rule: power the Pi from the buck, or from USB-C,
  never both.** Same conclusion V2 reached.
- **Total budget still needs confirming against the 5.5 A module.** Pi 5 + two USB cameras +
  SIM7600G-H (~2 A transmit bursts) + Teensy + LED + pod can approach it. Fusing contains faults; it
  does not create headroom.
- **`A2` thermal**: ~3 W dissipation at 5 A out from 18 V in. Needs airflow or a heatsink in layout.

### 17.2 The correction that shaped this pass

**Flipping a through-hole part to the back frees height, not area.** Its holes still occupy the same
X/Y. The first attempt put the divider chain on the back *underneath* the front pull-up bank and
produced 101 shorting violations. Back-side placement is therefore used only where **height** is the
constraint, and front/back regions are given distinct X/Y.

That leaves exactly one part on the back: **C30**, the 470 µF Pi bulk cap, in the dead strip above
the header inside the Pi shadow. A 20 mm can cannot stand there on the front under a Pi; on the back
it has unlimited room and still sits ~10 mm from J12's 5 V pins.

The corollary: **board size is set by total THT pad area regardless of side.** 162 x 123 is near the
floor for 39 axial resistors and 28 THT caps. Moving passives to 0805 is the only large lever left,
and it trades against the THT-for-reworkability decision.

### 17.7 Passive review — applied (2026-08-05)

Four passes over the 76 passives/discretes only (sensors explicitly out of scope). Headline:
**the passive set was already lean — only three parts didn't earn their place.** The real
problems were one wrong value, four *missing* parts, and a fragmented BOM.

**Value defect — R3/R5 56k → 68k.** The elec and winch dividers were 56k/10k = 0.1515, giving
3.18 V at a 21 V pack against a 3.3 V ADC — 3.6 % headroom. At 5 % tolerance the worst case is
53.2k/10.5k → **3.46 V, over the rail**. Now 68k/10k → 2.69 V nominal, **2.74 V at 1 % worst
case, 17 % headroom**. Same part count, same BOM line count; costs 15 % of ADC range, which is
irrelevant for a value read to 0.05 V.

**Deleted (3):**

| | Why |
|---|---|
| C23 10 µF on +5V | The Pololu module has its own output ceramics and C30 gives 470 µF at the Pi. A 10 µF aluminium at ~2 Ω ESR adds nothing either already covers. |
| C27 100 nF on WINCH_SW | Parallel to C26's 470 µF feeding an ESC with its own several-hundred-µF bank. Noise against that. |
| R37 1k into Q2's gate | No DC function; R36's pull-down is the fail-safe. Teensy now drives Q2's gate directly. The one I'd least defend — it was cheap ESD insurance. |

**Added (4) — worth more than anything deleted.** Each was missing protection on a net that
leaves the board:

| | Net | Job |
|---|---|---|
| R45 330 Ω | `LED_DIN` ↔ `LED_DIN_EXT` | WS2812 data damping over a cable run |
| R46 1k | `LEAK_L` ↔ `LEAK_L_EXT` | hull-leak probe — bare wire in bilge water onto a GPIO |
| R47 1k | `LEAK_R` ↔ `LEAK_R_EXT` | same |
| R48 1k | `SBUS` ↔ `SBUS_EXT` | 5 V-logic receiver into a 3.3 V-only pin; limits ESD-clamp current to ~1.1 mA |

Convention: the Teensy keeps the original net name, the connector side gets `_EXT`, so the
exposed conductor is obvious in the netlist. **Series resistor, not a divider** — a divider
sized for a 5 V receiver would drop a 3.3 V receiver below the Teensy's V<sub>IH</sub>.

**Specification.** Every passive now carries its spec in Value: **100nF 50V X7R** (a generic
disc could be Y5V and lose 80 % of its capacitance over temperature and DC bias), **22pF 50V
C0G**, **1 %** on every resistor. Twenty parts gained a `Description` so the intent stripped
out of Value isn't lost.

**BOM de-fragmentation.** Descriptions in the Value field were splitting identical parts across
order lines — 10 k appeared as four separate lines (`10k`, `10k gate pull-up`, `10k gate drive`,
`10k Q2 pull-down`), 470 µF 35 V as three. **28 BOM lines → 20** with no circuit change.

Net effect: 103 → **104 components**, 76 → 77 passives, **28 → 20 BOM lines**.

**Verification.** ERC clean (same four expected classes). Board synced without disturbing
placement or routing — all 59 track/via items preserved, **448 pads cross-checked, zero net
mismatches**, no new DRC violations.

⚠ R47 and R48 landed 52 mm and 38 mm from J9/J7 — the free-space search had nowhere closer.
They limit fault current from anywhere in series, but for ESD they want to sit at the
connector. Drag them when convenient.

⚠ Not adopted from the review: F2/F3 (the LED and SBUS PTCs) were left in place, and the
BNO085 crystal was kept rather than switching to the internal oscillator.

## 18. UPDATE 13 — first review of the *layout*; power copper fixed (2026-08-30)

**Method.** `kicad-cli pcb drc` for ground truth, plus `pcbnew` imported into the Python that ships
in KiCad's `bin/` for everything DRC cannot see. The important one: **netclass track widths are
defaults for newly drawn tracks, not DRC constraints.** Only `winch track width floor` in the

source to each real load**, not by eyeballing widths.

§17.6 netclass table), `+5V` **5.5 A** (the Pololu module's ceiling), and the PTC **hold** currents

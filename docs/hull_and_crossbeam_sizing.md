# D.R.E.A.M.S V2 — Hull, Bridge and Propulsion Sizing

Sizing for the V2 boat, modelled on the EvoLogics Sonobot 5 USV: two pontoon hulls and an orange
gull-wing bridge that arches from hull to hull. Everything is generated by stdlib-Python scripts in
`mechanical/`:

| Script | What it does |
|---|---|
| `hull_sizing.py` | Hull geometry, hydrostatics, buoyancy, mass budget, arch-frame and lid-joint loads (`--beam`, `--compare` try other hull widths) |
| `propulsion.py` | Resistance, propeller diameter and drive selection (`--beam` for other widths, `--selftest` checks the wave integral) |
| `drawings.py` | 7-sheet dimensioned drawing set, SolidWorks curves, and the arch-frame DXF |
| `concept_model.py` | 3D concept model: interactive viewer, still renders, OBJ mesh |
| `toroidal_prop.py` | Parametric toroidal prop for the belt outdrive's 5 mm shaft (or `--hub m200`) + thrust stand |
| `build_all.py` | Runs everything; see `mechanical/README.md` for how to change the design |

Change a parameter, re-run `build_all.py`, and update these tables. To model the boat, follow
`docs/solidworks_modeling_guide.md` alongside `mechanical/drawings/DREAMS_V2_drawings.pdf`.

**Status:** proposal, rev C, 2026-09-27. Nothing here is locked. See the decisions and open questions at the end.

## Design

### Demi-hulls (×2, identical)

| Parameter | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Size | **1300 × 220 × 250 mm**, 255 mm over the rail | Sonobot length |
| Section | 12° V bottom, **R50 chine**, vertical sides, **R50 deck edge** | Rounded-pontoon look. Glass cloth lies over the radii |
| Bow | Parallel to 650, taper to a 10 mm stem flat; keel rocker from 910 to +100 at the stem | A cut-away forefoot rides over weed and mud |
| Stern | Keel rises straight to +40 mm at the transom, from 520 fwd | Cp 0.71; the immersed transom is only 54 mm deep |
| Construction | **Open tub + removable lid**, joined at a horizontal joint plane **190 mm** above the baseline (96 mm above the design waterline) | Full-length access to the inside; see the lid joint below |
| Tub | 5 segments × 260 mm, each printed standing on its aft bulkhead, 8 mm spigot joints, glassed outside. Bulkheads stop 5 mm under the joint plane | Bare sealed float: no hardware, no holes below the rail |
| Lid | 5 segments × 260 mm (deck, deck-edge radius, 10 mm skirt), printed on end. Segments 3 and 4 stand on 8 mm ribs at J2 / J3, right under the arch frames | The ribs carry the bridge into the rail |
| Mass / volume | 4.1 kg tub + lid and 1.0 kg lid joint per hull; 51.6 L enclosed | |

**PLA is out.** It softens at 55–60 °C, and a dark hull in sun gets that hot. Use PETG or ASA.

### Lid joint (the lid goes over the gunwale)

The lid laps over the tub like a shoebox lid, but on a flange rather than a friction skirt. A friction
skirt can't be made to seal over 2.85 m of printed edge; a flange clamped on a gasket can.

| Part | Size (from the hull side, outboard +) | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Tub flange | 15 mm out × 5 mm, under the joint plane; Ø10 bosses under it at each screw | Carries the M4 × 8.1 brass heat-set inserts |
| Gasket | 3 mm EPDM or silicone cord in a **5 × 2.2 mm groove**, 1.5–6.5 mm from the hull side | Squeezed 27 % when the flanges touch: the groove sets the squeeze, so the joint can't be over-tightened, and the hard flange contact keeps the joint stiff |
| Lid flange | 15 mm × 5 mm, on the joint plane | Screw heads bear on it |
| Drip lip | 2.5 mm, from the lid flange's edge down 8 mm below the plane | Covers the joint from spray; locates the lid |
| Locating spigot | 6 × 2 mm under the lid, 0.3 mm inside the tub wall | Aligns the lid; a second barrier |
| Screws | **M4 × 12 A4 pan head at 10.5 mm**, outboard of the gasket: **37 per hull** (100 mm pitch, 60 mm within 150 mm of each frame) | The holes are on the wet side of the cord, so they don't need sealing |

Each hull is now one sealed space. Put a leak sensor at the lowest point of each tub (Boat PCB J15 /
J16 already exist) and a membrane vent in each lid. If you want the old bay-by-bay damage tolerance
back, wedge closed-cell foam blocks into the bays you don't use.

### Arched bridge

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Structure | **Two 1/4" 6061-T6 arch frame plates**, laser or waterjet cut from `mechanical/cad/arch_frame_plate.dxf` |
| Frame geometry | 50 mm deep, 750 × 112 mm blank, 0.50 kg each. They stand in the transverse plane at x = **520 and 780 mm**, over the lid ribs at J2 and J3 |
| Arch | Straight hub span at z 345 between y ±170, then a cubic Bezier to vertical feet on the hull centrelines (±350) at z 257.5 |
| Arms | Printed PETG/ASA fairings, 300 mm wide fore-aft, flaring into the hub. Each is also the cable duct to its hull |
| Hub | Lens-shaped printed cowl, **560 × 400 mm**, z 310–550, centred **610 mm** fwd of the transom (level trim). It houses a **480 × 300 × 120 mm IP67 box** on the frame tops |
| In the hub | 4 M18 packs lying flat (2 drive, electronics, winch), 2 VESCs, Pi 5, Boat PCB, Picos, SIM7600, GPS, IMU, RC receiver, and the winch. The pod hangs under the hub on its tether |
| On top | GNSS puck on a mast to **820 mm**, LTE and WiFi whips, e-stop, and the stereo camera bar (320 baseline) on the front of the dome |
| Clearance | Hub underside **216 mm above the waterline**. A stowed pod (≤ 160 mm tall) stays clear of the water |
| Feet | A 300 × 140 × 6 mm 6061 plate on each side, bolted to both frames with angle brackets. It sits on the lid deck on a 1.5 mm EPDM pad (the pad also takes up the 0.9 mm fall of the deck radius under the foot's edges) |
| Joint | **8× M6 × 60 A4 per foot, from inside the lid.** At each frame, 4 bolts come up through full-depth Ø20 bosses in the lid rib (10 mm fwd of the frame), heads in 13 × 8 mm counterbores on bonded-seal washers, through the deck and pad, into tapped holes in the foot. The counterbores mean the rib is never notched. No latches, no dowels |

The bridge, both lids and both outdrives form one **upper module**. To open a hull you undo its rail
screws and lift the upper module. The trade is that every opening re-makes the seal, so keep the cords
clean and replace any that take a set.

### Propulsion and power

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Drive | **V1-style belt outdrive**, one per hull, bolted to the lid's aft deck (4× M5 from inside) |
| Motor | ~380 KV 4108-size outrunner above the deck, under a printed cover. Its axis runs fore-aft at z 300 |
| Belt | **1:1, HTD 3M, 9 mm, 209 teeth (627 mm)**, 20T pulleys (19.1 PD), centres 283 mm. The slotted motor plate tensions it |
| Leg and pod | Streamlined leg 80 × 28 mm (the two belt runs are 19 mm apart inside it), leading edge 28 mm aft of the transom, clear of the rail. Ø32 lower pod with the lower pulley and 2× 625-2RS stainless bearings on a 5 mm 316 shaft |
| Prop | 110 mm, axis z 17 mm, tip 22 mm under the design waterline. Toroidal (`toroidal_3loop_p75_RH_shaft` / `LH`) or a 110 mm RC boat prop on the same D-shaft, M5 nylock |
| Kick-up | The whole drive tilts about a transverse pin at (x −30, z 262), like an outboard. The motor tilts with the leg, so the belt tension doesn't change. A spring-plunger detent holds it down against reverse thrust |
| Total draft | ~132 mm at design load |
| Drive power | **One M18 HD12.0 per VESC, 18 V** (unchanged) |

### Drive train: belt outdrive vs M200 direct drive

| | Belt outdrive (baseline) | M200 + weedless |
|---|---|---|
| Parts | Hobby outrunners, pulleys, belts, shafts, bearings, hardware; printed parts | Two M200 thrusters and a prop set |
| Power, both drives @ 1.0 / 1.5 / 3.0 m/s | 22 / 89 / 359 W | 21 / 82 / 330 W |
| Drive endurance @ 1.0 / 1.5 m/s | 17 h / 4.4 h | 18 h / 4.7 h |
| Motor | Dry, above the deck, cheap hobby outrunner | Wet, flooded, marine-rated |
| In the water | A leg and pod; the belt and bearings run wet | Just the motor pod and prop |
| Maintenance | Rinse after use; belt and bearings are consumables | Rinse after use |

- **1:1 is enough.** At 18 V a 380 KV motor turns 6,840 rpm no-load. The prop wants ~1,660 rpm at
  1.5 m/s and ~3,880 rpm at 3 m/s, so the motor runs at 25–55 % of no-load, its comfortable range. A
  reduction would add parts and not unlock a bigger prop, because the draft limits diameter.
- **Belt rating:** at 3 m/s each drive carries ~0.38 N·m at ~3,900 rpm. That is near a 9 mm HTD 3M
  belt's rating on a 20T pulley. Use a 15 mm belt if you plan long fast runs; cruise is well inside it.
- **Wet belt:** use a polyurethane belt with steel or aramid cords (neoprene/glass belts swell and
  weaken), 316 or 440C stainless bearings, and rinse with fresh water after each run.
- **M200 stays the premium option.** It is quieter, with nothing to wear in the water and ~8 % less power.
  `toroidal_prop.py --hub m200` still makes props that fit it.

### Toroidal prop experiment

- **Generator:** `mechanical/toroidal_prop.py` makes parametric looped-blade props. The default hub
  is now 20 mm OD × 43 long with a **D-bore for the outdrive's 5 mm shaft** (4.5 across the flat),
  held by an M5 nylock. `--hub m200` switches to the M200 cup (measured from Blue Robotics' CAD).
  The default is 3 loops, 110 mm, pitch 75, ~16 g of resin.
- **Printing:** print them on the EXP Form 3L in tough resin.
- **Testing:** compare against a 110 mm RC prop on the rig in `mechanical/props/thrust_stand.pdf`
  (thrust per watt at ~5, 10 and 25 N, plus a weed-wrap test).
- **Expectations:** benefits at this size are unproven. Sharrow's 9–15 % claim comes from full-size
  outboard props, so keep a plain prop in the kit until a toroidal one wins on the stand.

## Bridge and joint loads (design condition)

| Case | Frame moment | Frame stress | SF on yield |
|---|---|---|---|
| LC1 afloat, 2.5 g (slam, rough launch) | 28 N·m | 11 MPa | 26 |
| LC2 lifted by one hull ×1.5 (bank launch or recovery) | 112 N·m | 43 MPa | 6.5 |
| **LC3 hulls pitching out of phase, a_cg 2 g** | **136 N·m** | **52 MPa** | **5.4** |
| LC4 whole boat carried by the hub ×2 | 16 N·m | 6 MPa | 45 |

| Joint (worst case LC3) | Load | Capacity | SF |
|---|---|---|---|
| Foot bolts, M6 (8 per foot, in the rib bosses) | 688 N on the worst bolt | 2,400 N (washer ring bearing on PETG; the bolt itself ~9 kN) | 3.5 |
| Lid rib under each frame, 8 mm PETG, 60 deep, never notched | 10 MPa | 45 MPa | 4.7 |
| Rail screws beside a frame (3 share the rib's reaction) | 254 N each | 1,200 N (M4 insert pull-out) | 4.7 |

- **Load path:** each frame is a portal fixed at its feet. The foot pivots on its far edge and the
  bolts hold the near side down. The lid rib carries that couple out to the rail, and the rail
  screws beside the rib take it into the tub, right over the tub's J2 / J3 bulkheads.
- **Weak points:** the printed bosses and heat-set inserts, not the plates or bolts. **Pull-test one
  boss and one insert from the actual printer** before building the lids.

## Resistance and drive selection

The resistance build-up (`propulsion.py`) sums:

- ITTC-57 friction × form factor 1.25
- Michell wave resistance with twin-hull interference (the self-test reproduces the Wigley curve)
- Holtrop transom resistance
- outdrive legs and pods (25 cm² frontal each, Cd 0.35) and air drag
- a 15 % service margin

| V (m/s) | Fn | Total drag (N) | R/W |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | 0.28 | 6.4 | 0.020 |
| 1.5 | 0.42 | 20.5 | 0.065 |
| 2.0 | 0.56 | 32.9 | 0.105 |
| 3.0 | 0.84 | 46.8 | 0.149 |

| Drive (2 per boat) | 1.0 m/s | 1.5 m/s | 3.0 m/s | Endurance @ 1.0 / 1.5 m/s | Total draft |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Belt outdrive + 110 prop** | 22 W | 89 W | 359 W | 17 h / 4 h | 132 mm |
| M200 + 112 weedless | 21 W | 82 W | 330 W | 18 h / 5 h | 134 mm |
| T200 ducted | 25 W | 104 W | 406 W | 16 h / 4 h | 115 mm |

- **Energy:** 389 Wh of drive energy from 2× HD12.0.
- **Electronics limit:** at an estimated 11–12 W (Pi 5 running vision + LTE), the XC5.0 pack gives
  ~7 h. So below ~1.3 m/s the electronics pack, not the drive, ends the mission.
- **Top speed:** each outdrive needs ~180 W at 3 m/s: ~15 A of motor current (~10 A from the pack),
  inside a 4108-size motor's rating.
- **Accuracy:** treat the power numbers as ±30 % until a tow test. The outdrive's efficiency (κ 0.46)
  and bollard thrust (~55 N each) are estimates until it is run on the thrust stand.

**VESC setup:** motor current max ~25 A (check the motor's rating), battery current max ~20 A per
VESC, FOC detection with the prop in the water. Hobby outrunners are rated to 6S (25 V), so fresh
M18 packs at 21 V need no duty limit.

## Printing on the EXP Ultimaker S5

- **Tub segments** print standing on their aft bulkhead: 255 × 190 section (over the rail), 260 tall.
- **Lid segments** print on end the same way: 255 × 68 section, 260 tall. Lid segments 3 and 4 stand
  on their ribs, with the four bolt bosses growing up off the bed.
- That's 20 prints for two hulls. The open sections print faster than rev B's closed ones.
- **Bridge parts:** the hub cowl (560 × 400) and the arm fairings are bigger than the bed. Print them
  in pieces and bond them. They're fairings only: the IP67 box does the sealing and the aluminium
  frames carry the load.
- **Outdrive parts** (bracket, cover, leg halves, pod) are small; print them in ASA.
- **Before committing:** ask the makerspace about multi-day prints and PETG/ASA. Their page lists
  PLA and ABS only.

## Method and limits

- Hydrostatics are integrated over 800 stations, including the chine and deck fillets.
- Michell's integral runs on the unfilleted lines (the fillets change wave drag by < 2 %). The transom
  is treated as dry, as Michlet does. This hull is fat (L/B 5.8), so the hump is probably
  overpredicted, which is on the safe side.
- The prop model is momentum theory with a loss factor (0.50 M200, calibrated against Blue Robotics'
  bollard data; 0.46 belt outdrive, estimated). Power numbers are ±30 %.
- Frames are checked as portals fixed at the feet with simple beam theory. The foot joint is a bolt
  group pivoting on the foot's far edge; the lid rib is a beam between the rail screw lines. Load
  factors live in `Loads`, joint ratings in `Lid` and `Boat`.
- The rev A bridge used straight 1" square crossbeams with pin saddles. It is still a valid, lighter
  fallback if the arch proves hard to build.

## Open questions

1. **Battery layout:** all four M18 packs sit in the hub (Matthew: main electronics in the bridge).
   Now that the hulls open, the drive packs could ride low in the tubs instead: lower CG, a lighter
   bridge, shorter motor cables. They would then be in the "hull" half of the joint.
2. **Drive bus:** approve one M18 per VESC at 18 V. This changes the GP26 divider (200k/10k → 56k/10k),
   the thresholds (15–21 V), the fuses (~40 A per VESC) and the wiring. The second drive pack is read
   through VESC telemetry.
3. **Makerspace:** are 20–40 h prints and PETG/ASA allowed on the S5?
4. **Pod:** V1-style box assumed at 110 × 80 × 160 mm, 1.2 kg. Measure the real one.
5. **Hub box:** find an IP67 box near 480 × 300 × 120 mm, or resize the cowl
   (`Boat.box` / `hub_half`; the script checks the fit).
6. **Electronics pack:** measure the real draw. At more than ~10 W, missions are capped near 7 h.
7. **Hull width:** stay at 220 mm, or go narrower? See the buoyancy table; 6 in needs new feet.
8. **Outdrive parts:** pick the motor (the size and KV above) and confirm κ and bollard thrust on the
   thrust stand. Reuse V1's pulleys and belt length if they fit.
9. **Printed joints:** pull-test a lid-rib boss and a flange insert before printing the lids.
